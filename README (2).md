# **LED USING LDR**

## **1\. Aim**

To design and build a simple **automatic LED control circuit using an LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)**, in which the LED automatically turns ON or OFF depending on the surrounding light intensity.

## **2\. Problem Statement**

In conventional lighting systems, lights are manually switched ON and OFF. This can lead to unnecessary power consumption when lights remain ON during daylight.

To solve this problem, an **LDR** can be used to detect the intensity of surrounding light and automatically control an LED.

## **3\. Proposed Solution**

The proposed system uses an **LDR, LED, resistor, and transistor** to create an automatic light-control circuit.

The resistance of the LDR changes according to the intensity of light falling on it.

* In bright light → LDR resistance decreases → LED remains OFF.  
* In darkness → LDR resistance increases → LED turns ON.

Thus, the LED can be automatically controlled based on the surrounding light.

## **4\. Components Required**

1. LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)  
2. LED  
3. Resistor  
4. Transistor  
5. Breadboard  
6. Battery / DC Power Supply  
7. Jumper Wires

## **5\. Circuit Working**

The LDR acts as a light sensor.

* When **light falls on the LDR**, its resistance decreases.  
* This changes the voltage in the control circuit and keeps the LED **OFF**.  
* When the surrounding light decreases, the resistance of the LDR increases.  
* The change in voltage activates the transistor and the LED **turns ON**.

Therefore, the LED automatically responds to changes in surrounding light intensity.

## **6\. Procedure**

1. Place the LDR, LED, resistor, and transistor on the breadboard.  
2. Connect the LDR to the control section of the circuit.  
3. Connect the LED with the correct polarity.  
4. Connect the resistor to limit the LED current.  
5. Connect the transistor to control the LED.  
6. Connect the power supply to the circuit.  
7. Check all connections carefully.  
8. Switch ON the power supply.  
9. Expose the LDR to bright light and observe the LED.  
10. Cover the LDR and observe the change in LED operation.

## **7\. Applications**

* 💡 Automatic street lights  
* 🏠 Automatic home lighting  
* 🌙 Night lamps  
* 🚗 Automatic vehicle lighting systems  
* 🌱 Garden lighting systems  
* 🏫 Educational electronics projects

## **8\. Advantages**

* Automatic operation  
* Simple and low-cost circuit  
* Saves electrical energy  
* No manual switching required  
* Easy to construct  
* Suitable for beginners  
* Responds automatically to changes in light intensity

## **9\. Result**

The **LED using LDR circuit** was successfully constructed and tested. The LED automatically responded to changes in surrounding light intensity, turning **ON in darkness and OFF in bright light**.

## **10\. Conclusion**

The project successfully demonstrates the use of an **LDR as a light sensor for automatic LED control**. It provides a practical understanding of **light-dependent resistance, sensing, transistor switching, and automatic lighting control**.
